EVMS Training

Earned value management and EVMS—interactive lessons, metrics drills, and ten project scenarios.

This page is an interactive training experience used by program controls, finance, and engineering teams to practice earned value management (EVM) and EVMS-style reporting (PV, EV, AC, BAC, variances, SPI/CPI, and simple EAC/VAC forecasts). When JavaScript loads successfully, you can choose learning paths, take a short quiz, and drive live metrics through narrative scenarios.

No JavaScript? You can still read everything on this screen for procurement reviews, accessibility, or locked-down browsers. If scripts are only blocked temporarily, refresh once your connection allows the application bundle to load—the same headings and flows appear in interactive mode.

Learning paths (recommended order)

PDU / contact hours: This interactive practice tool supplements formal training; contact hours and PDUs depend on your certifying body (PMI, etc.). Completion certificates (PDF with name/date) are available in interactive mode after finishing each path.

1. EVMS 101 — terms, comparison, and quiz

Estimated time: 45–60 minutes.

Introduces BAC, PV, EV, AC, schedule and cost variances (SV, CV), indices (SPI, CPI), and forecast concepts (EAC, ETC, VAC). Includes a contrast between spend-only tracking and true earned value, a small numeric example, and a short multiple-choice quiz. Aligned with EIA-748 (ANSI-748) intent and the PMI Practice Standard for Earned Value Management.

Learning objectives:

  • Define BAC, PV, EV, AC and contrast them with spend-only tracking
  • Calculate SV, CV, SPI, and CPI from a status snapshot
  • Score 6+ of 8 on the closing quiz (75%+)

Teaser examples: Mini walkthrough BAC $100k / PV $40k / EV $35k / AC $38k; quiz on which metric measures schedule in budget units.

2. Read the metrics — twelve scenario drills

Estimated time: 2–3 hours.

Twelve fixed snapshots with realistic program narratives. Each case shows PV, EV, AC, and BAC so you can interpret SPI, CPI, and variances. Several items include a program manager quote that may conflict with the numbers—you choose the audit-safe response. Covers risk/opportunity management, baseline change control, and IPMR-style reporting ties.

Teaser examples: Firmware milestone—PM says “green” but SPI below 1.0; subcontractor overrun with baseline change on the table.

3. Project scenarios — ten choose-your-path projects

Estimated time: 4–6 hours (all ten scenarios).

Hands-on stories from a backyard fence through a lunar mission program. Decisions change planned value, earned value, and actual cost; the dashboard updates SPI/CPI and related metrics. Progress is saved in the browser (local storage) so you can resume across tabs and sessions when storage is available.

Teaser examples: Building a Fence—post-setting vs rush and live SPI; Lunar Mission—scope creep vs formal baseline change under customer IPMR scrutiny.

All scenario titles:

  • Building a Fence, Kitchen Renovation, Software Feature Sprint
  • Marketing Campaign Launch, Office Relocation, Manufacturing Line Setup
  • Hospital Wing Expansion, Bridge Rehabilitation, New Product Launch, Lunar Mission Program

Metrics you will practice

PV, EV, AC, BAC, EAC, ETC, SV, CV, SPI, CPI, and VAC—aligned with EIA-748 (ANSI-748) and the PMI Practice Standard for Earned Value Management. Narratives emphasize that schedule variance is in budget units (valued work), not calendar days by itself.

Authoritative references (external)

  • PMI — Practice Standard for Earned Value Management
  • NDIA — Earned Value Management Systems (EIA-748 intent materials)
  • NASA — EVM portal and handbooks

Audience

Program managers, control account managers, financial analysts, engineers, and auditors who need a low-risk practice environment before touching production systems or customer deliverables.
